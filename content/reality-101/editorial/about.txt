*Reality 101* is a podcast series Steven Schrembeck made with his friend Joshua Swenson. Its episodes were published between May and October 2026. Steven called it "a journal of impossible experiences." He died in September 2026.

This book holds the whole series in the order he released it, the three solo audio journals he added as bonus episodes, and selections from four interviews he gave before the series began. Around those chapters are pages written by his friend Jeff Cameron, who edited the book.

## His words

Every sentence in the body of a chapter was spoken by the person it is attributed to. The text was made from transcripts of the recordings by cutting only. Filler, stutters, false starts, repeated explanations, cross-talk, recording logistics and show promotion were removed. Punctuation, capitalization and paragraph breaks are editorial. No word was added, replaced or moved; where Steven says "gonna," the page says "gonna." A program checks each paragraph against the transcript, word by word and in order, every time the book is built.

Chapter titles follow the episode titles. Section headings are editorial. The few explanations placed between passages are labelled **Editor's context**. Joshua Swenson's questions and reflections, and those of the interview hosts, appear under their own names, shortened by the same method.

The series and the journals are nearly complete: they keep what Steven said and remove how speech stumbles. The interviews are selections. What was left out of each chapter is described under *Sources and editorial note* at the back.

## The editor's pages

The preface, the short introductions before the chapters, the pages headed *connections and questions* after them, and the essays in Part Four are Jeff Cameron's writing, not Steven's. They are set apart from the chapters and marked with his name. When they quote Steven, the quotation is checked by the same program against the chapter it comes from. When they draw on another author, the source is listed under *Works cited*. They were drafted with AI assistance.

## What to keep in mind

The transcripts were made by machine and have not been fully checked against the audio by a listener. A machine can mishear a word, and a cut can change an emphasis. Where a transcribed word looked wrong, it was left as transcribed or the clause was left out; it was not replaced with a guess. The spelling of a small number of names and terms was corrected, and each correction is recorded. A list of about two hundred places where the transcript looks doubtful, with the time of each in its recording, is kept with the book's files for anyone willing to listen and check. Anything that matters to you is worth hearing in his voice: every chapter's recording and full transcript are in the Steven Schrembeck Archive, linked from the notes at the back.

This edition was prepared after Steven's death. He did not review or approve it.

The chapters are his accounts of his own experience. They neither argue for those accounts nor against them, and they keep his doubts beside his certainty. The editor's pages do have a point of view, and say where it is.

## How to read it

Start with the *Invitation* and the *Prologue*; he called the prologue required listening for everything after it. Parts One and Two then follow his own order. Part Three steps back to the interviews he gave before the series, mostly to people who had asked him about technology. Part Four is the editor's.

*A reader's guide* at the back gathers some of the statements he returned to, an index of the practices he describes for trying it yourself, and a list of the experiences he recounts, each linked to its place in the text.
