# Abilities and the state of the evidence

Dossier for the editor of Reality 101. Compiled 11 November 2026.

Scope: the abilities Steven says he trained (telepathy, remote viewing, "mindsight", the psi wheel, metal bending), his claims about belief, doubt and groups, the levitation accounts, and the food lesson: what others report, what the best studies found, what the strongest critics say.

Conventions. Chapter references are file number plus block id (03 B0275) in `/home/user/steven-schrembeck-memorial/content/reality-101/chapters/`. Steven's words are exact. Source quotations are words I saw on a fetched page or in a search result. UNVERIFIED marks a detail not re-confirmed in this session. The shared web-search allowance ran out near the end, so some secondary details (listed under Open questions) were not chased. The file exceeds the brief's length target because the assignment covers ten areas.

One caution throughout: nearly all positive results come from researchers who expect psi to exist and appear in parapsychology journals (Journal of Scientific Exploration, Journal of Parapsychology, Explore); nearly all negative commentary comes from committed skeptics. Little work has been done by people with no stake.

## Strongest parallels

1. **The fork at the retreat and Jack Houck's "PK parties" (close).** Steven: "I just walked up and did it in front of a group of five or six people who were all very excited, and they all believed it was possible, and then they did it" (05 B0211). From January 1981 the engineer Jack Houck ran some 360 gatherings on this pattern: raise group excitement, command the cutlery to bend, let go; he reported about 85 percent success. Michael Crichton attended one and wrote that the metal became "completely pliable, like soft plastic" and that "almost anyone could do it". The skeptical account (people bend the metal by hand while excited and misjudge the force) fits both cases equally.

2. **"Turns out like a light" and Kenneth Batcheldor's theory of belief (close).** Steven: "you just get somebody in the room who thinks that you're crazy, or they're judging. They think you're dumb. Turns out like a light" (03 B0295); of his own doubt, "Bam. It's off" (03 B0299). Batcheldor, an English clinical psychologist who ran table-tilting groups from the 1960s, held that what matters is "the balance of belief over doubt at the very instant when a PK event is about to occur", and recommended chatter and laughter to stop analysis, which matches Steven's "generally easier in groups" (08 B0090). The same idea makes the claim very hard to test.

3. **How telepathy feels: Steven and René Warcollier (close on phenomenology).** Steven says most of what arrives is self-generated ("assume that seventy to ninety percent of the information is just you talking to yourself", 07 B0099), that it passes through the receiver "like a pachinko machine or like filters" (07 B0019), and that reception is clearest "if you can almost fall asleep" (07 B0100). Warcollier, from drawing-transmission trials begun in the 1920s, reported the same: impressions arrive fragmentary and transformed, they "spring exclusively from his own mind", and sleep-onset states are best. Remote-viewing training, which Steven took, teaches the same point as "analytical overlay", so this is partly shared training, not wholly independent.

4. **"Easier for everyone else" and Rupert Sheldrake's morphic resonance (close in content).** Steven: "every time somebody does something like this, I think it's easier for everyone else to do it" (05 B0206); "no matter where you are, it seems to get easier for everyone else" (05 B0211). Sheldrake's 1981 hypothesis is that whatever has happened becomes more probable everywhere, without contact. Its popular form, the "hundredth monkey", was withdrawn by its author. Steven does not name Sheldrake.

5. **Open versus cynical skeptics and the sheep-goat effect (moderate).** Steven: "you can be skeptical, but you have to be sort of open and positive and curious with your skepticism. If you are cynical or combative, nothing happens" (13 B0116). Gertrude Schmeidler found in the 1940s that people who accept ESP score slightly above those who reject it. Meta-analyses put the difference at about 0.03, a tilt in averages; Steven describes a switch.

6. **Seeing with eyes closed and "eyeless sight" (close as a claim; direct lineage).** Steven: "I can close my eyes, put on a blindfold, and see like I have eyes" (01 B0185). Jules Romains made the same claim with experiments in 1920; the Bronnikov school in Russia and today's "mindsight" trainers teach it, and Steven learned from that community. Each documented test in which light leaks were fully closed ended the effect.

7. **The psi-wheel progression and Sean McNamara's manual (direct influence, named).** Steven's stages (open wheel, "under a big bell glass jar", then "from a few feet away", 03 B0275) are those of the book he read, whose real title is Defy Your Limits: The Telekinesis Training Method.

8. **Food carrying the quality of how it was made (close to Hindu teaching; weak in the laboratory).** Nom: "The food is trying to give you its entire experience at every step of its own experience" (09 B0006). A widespread Hindu belief holds that "the consciousness of the cook enters the food and influences the mind of the eater". Steven says the idea was new to him (09 B0027); he had read Dean Radin, whose group ran small trials of "intentionally enhanced" chocolate and tea.

## Verdicts by area

1. **Telepathy.** Proponents' meta-analyses keep finding a small excess (about 31 to 32 percent hits where 25 is chance); critics attribute it to selective reporting, flexible analysis and leaks; the main skeptic-run meta-analysis found nothing; no result is accepted by mainstream science. Dream telepathy succeeded in one laboratory and failed independent replication. The Telepathy Tapes offers unblinded demonstrations, not tests.
2. **Remote viewing.** Both official reviewers in 1995 agreed the laboratory data departed from chance and disagreed about what that meant; the programme was closed as operationally useless; the 2023 replication is partial, not preregistered and not independently repeated.
3. **Mindsight.** There is no controlled demonstration; where the blindfold was made fully light-tight the ability stopped; a research programme was proposed in 2023 and I found no published results.
4. **The psi wheel.** An uncovered wheel is turned by warm air from the hands and body; a glass cover reduces but does not remove heat-driven air movement; one instrumented study claims motion beyond this, in parapsychology journals, unreplicated.
5. **Group metal bending.** Thousands have sincerely experienced cutlery going soft at such gatherings; nobody has shown metal bending without ordinary force under controlled observation, and children secretly watched used hands and feet.
6. **Belief and doubt.** A very small statistical link between belief and ESP scores recurs in proponents' meta-analyses; nothing shows that doubt switches off a real ability, and the claim as Steven states it resists falsification.
7. **Morphic resonance.** A hypothesis with no independently replicated support; tests are disputed or negative.
8. **Levitation.** Abundant sworn early-modern testimony, no instrumented modern case; Eire argues the testimony must be taken seriously as history and does not plainly assert that it happened.
9. **Food and blessing.** The tradition is ancient and broad; laboratory evidence that intention changes food is three small studies from one group; there is good ordinary evidence that perceived care and ritual change how food tastes.
10. **Method.** Parapsychology adopted strict methods early, and when the strictest were applied to its best-known recent claim (Bem 2011) the effect vanished; proponents reply that the ganzfeld record survives bias corrections; the dispute is now as much about prior plausibility as data.

## Source notes

### bemhonorton1994
- Citation: Bem, D. J., and Honorton, C. (1994). Does psi exist? Replicable evidence for an anomalous process of information transfer. Psychological Bulletin 115(1), 4-18.
- Confirmed at: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/node/1696819 (archived copy); DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.115.1.4.
- What it reports: In the ganzfeld a "receiver" in mild sensory isolation describes imagery while a distant "sender" views a randomly chosen picture; the receiver then picks it from four. Reported hits: about 35 percent in 28 early studies and 32 percent in 10 computer-controlled studies (chance 25). The rationale is Honorton's "noise reduction" model (1977): psi as a weak signal best noticed in relaxed, dreamlike states.
- Parallel: Steven, 07 B0100: "If you can almost fall asleep, like taking a nap, that is when you get the clearest communication." Closeness: moderate. Difference: the ganzfeld tests a living person receiving a checkable picture; Steven says "I haven't done a lot of human-to-human telepathy" (07 B0014) and mostly describes contact with non-physical persons, which no ganzfeld study addresses.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no.
- Standing and criticism: Ray Hyman replied in the same issue that independent replication was needed. See the next two entries.

### miltonwiseman1999
- Citation: Milton, J., and Wiseman, R. (1999). Does psi exist? Lack of replication of an anomalous process of information transfer. Psychological Bulletin 125(4), 387-391.
- Confirmed at: https://researchprofiles.herts.ac.uk/en/publications/does-psi-exist-lack-of-replication-of-an-anomalous-process-of-inf/
- What it reports: Thirty later ganzfeld studies from 7 laboratories showed no significant effect (Stouffer z = 0.70). Conclusion: "the ganzfeld technique does not at present offer a replicable method for producing ESP in the laboratory".
- Parallel: none; the main published failure to confirm.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no.
- Standing and criticism: Proponents objected to the cut-off date and the pooling. Bem, Palmer and Broughton added ten later studies and reported significance restored, with a smaller effect (https://philpapers.org/rec/BEMUTG).

### tressoldistorm2024
- Citation: Tressoldi, P. E., and Storm, L. (2024). Stage 2 Registered Report: Anomalous perception in a Ganzfeld condition - A meta-analysis of more than 40 years investigation. F1000Research 10:234 (version 4). Earlier: Storm, L., Tressoldi, P. E., and Di Risio, L. (2010). Meta-analysis of free-response studies, 1992-2008. Psychological Bulletin 136(4), 471-485 (page range UNVERIFIED).
- Confirmed at: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11134153/ ; 2010 paper: DOI 10.1037/a0019457.
- What it reports: The most recent and most transparent proponent analysis: 78 studies, 1974-2020; overall effect about 0.08, a hit rate 6.8 points above chance; participants selected for prior experience or practice scored about three times higher than unselected ones; no decline over time; median statistical power only 0.088. The 2010 paper found 29 ganzfeld studies at 0.142, other relaxed-state studies at 0.110, and ordinary waking studies at slightly below zero.
- Parallel: relaxed states doing better than waking ones matches Steven's practice advice (07 B0100); the advantage of selected participants fits "aptitude is definitely a thing" (05 B0061). Closeness: moderate.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no.
- Standing and criticism: Open peer review of the 2024 report was split (one "Not Approved", two approvals including Dean Radin's). Rouder, Morey and Province (Psychological Bulletin, 2013) argued the 2010 case depends on studies with manual randomisation. Bierman, Spottiswoode and Bijl (PLoS ONE, 2016, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4856278), themselves psi researchers, simulated questionable research practices and concluded they inflate the result without wholly removing it. A critic can fairly say a literature of underpowered studies with a steady small excess is also what selective reporting produces.

### ullman1973
- Citation: Ullman, M., Krippner, S., with Vaughan, A. (1973). Dream Telepathy. Macmillan (publisher UNVERIFIED).
- Confirmed at: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_telepathy
- What it reports: At the Maimonides Medical Center dream laboratory in Brooklyn in the 1960s, sleepers woken from REM reported dreams while a distant agent concentrated on a picture; judges matched dreams to pictures. Ten experiments, most reported as significant.
- Parallel: Steven treats dreams and the edges of sleep as his best channel (07 B0011, B0100). Closeness: moderate. Difference: living senders and picture targets.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no.
- Standing and criticism: Belvedere and Foulkes (1971, Perceptual and Motor Skills 33, 783-789) failed to replicate; C. E. M. Hansel argued the target could have leaked through the experimenter; Sherwood and Roe (2003) pooled later studies and found small positive effects, which James Alcock dismissed for the data's "extreme messiness"; a later sleep-laboratory attempt by Caroline Watt and Richard Wiseman found nothing.

### cardena2018

### air1995
- Citation: Mumford, M. D., Rose, A. M., and Goslin, D. A. (1995). An Evaluation of Remote Viewing: Research and Applications. American Institutes for Research. With Utts, J. ### escolagascon2023
- Citation: Escolà-Gascón, Á., Houran, J., Dagnall, N., Drinkwater, K., and Denovan, A. (2023). Follow-up on the U.S. Central Intelligence Agency's (CIA) remote viewing experiments. Brain and Behavior 13(6), e3026.

### romains1920
- Citation: Romains, J. (1920). La Vision extra-rétinienne et le sens paroptique. English: Eyeless Sight, trans. C. K. Ogden (G. P. Putnam's Sons, 1924).

### bem2011

## What is distinctive in Steven's account

- **A practitioner's error budget.** He puts numbers on how much of his telepathy is himself (70 to 90 percent, 07 B0099), how often it is unmistakably other ("one in fifty", 07 B0098), and ranks channels by reliability, with words least and a sensation in the chest most (07 B0080-B0085). I found no other first-person account this frank or this specific.
- **Abilities as a gauge of the self.** The psi wheel is "a really good barometer for, how connected am I?" (03 B0307). The literature treats belief as a condition of the effect, not the effect as a reading of belief.
- **One mechanism for everything.** "That's why psi works. That's why remote viewing works. That's why levitation works... it only works because it's all you" (06 B0081). Parapsychology has no agreed theory.
- **Frequency as contextual goodness.** The definition at 09 B0019-B0020, the arsenic objection, and the instruction not to mislead people (09 B0022) are more careful than popular "high vibration" talk or Emoto.
- **Self-criticism inside the claim.** "I think I'm also looking at artifacts in my cornea" (01 B0235); "There is pareidolia, of course" (13 B0117); "I haven't bothered to engineer that test because I don't care" (03 B0262).
- **Mindsight without a blindfold**, including seeing his own eyelashes as "bright lines" (01 B0238).
- **Absent from his account:** scores. He mentions blind targets and many sessions but gives no hit rates, and no session in which the wheel was watched with nobody trying.

## What a careful skeptic would say

A fair skeptic grants what is not in dispute: Steven sold nothing, recorded failures, sought a psychiatric opinion, and by his account was changed for the better. The question is what caused the observations.

1. **Each has a known ordinary cause that was not excluded.** The wheel: warm air, including inside a jar; long sessions, no baseline. Mindsight: eyelids pass light and unmasked lids can part unnoticed; trained people can use extremely faint light (medvedev2005). Steven's details fit: glare from the sun still troubles him (01 B0215), he sees eyelashes (01 B0238), and the ability stops when he checks for peeking (03 B0299), which is also what happens if checking makes him shut his eyes fully (the eyelash point is my inference, not sourced). The live demonstration (03 B0338-B0361) was a mixed run: orange and black right, purple called blue, a final pair of orange and light pink called green and blue, with eyes closed, no mask, and feedback as it went. Metal: hands, excitement, misjudged force. Remote viewing: targets without ground truth, matches judged by the viewer. Telepathy: inner speech and sleep-onset imagery, which Steven agrees supply most of the content.
2. **The favouring conditions are the conditions in which errors go unnoticed:** belief, group excitement, no doubters, "focused inattention". If doubt switches the ability off, every failed test is explained in advance.
3. **The field's history points one way.** Effects shrink as controls tighten: the ganzfeld from 35 percent to about 31; Bem's 53 percent to 49.89 under an agreed protocol; eyeless sight to nothing once the mask is sealed; child metal-benders to hands and feet once secretly filmed.
4. **Asking again is not independent checking.** Steven's "error correction" (07 B0074) puts the question to the same mind; "eventual validation" (03 B0243) allows unlimited time and counts coincidences such as billboards (03 B0114-B0119).
5. **Change in the person is real without the mechanism being so.** Long practice, absorption and a supportive group alter attention, mood and conviction.

What the skeptic cannot say is that Steven's claims were tested and failed. They were never tested. Several could be, cheaply: a sealed jar filmed with nobody present for comparison; a sealed mask with someone else choosing the cards; the dated sunspot drawing against that day's solar image.
