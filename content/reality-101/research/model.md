# The model of reality: who else has said what Steven says

Dossier for the editor, checked 11 November 2026. Chapters 02, 04, 05 and 06 were read in full and the others searched for names. Steven's words are quoted exactly, with chapter number and block id. Outside sources are paraphrased; a direct quotation is under 25 words and was seen at the URL given.

Two things to know first:

- **Influence versus convergence.** Steven names three of the closest sources himself: the Seth books and the *Law of One* (07 @B0046; 06 @B0176) and the *Yoga Sutras* (01 @B0062; 06 @B0151). His own blog outline of the second lecture credits Seth. Those parallels show what he had read; they are not independent confirmation. The sources he does not name (the Hymn of the Pearl, Harding, the Upanishad passage, Vasubandhu, Leibniz) can be called independent, and they match in feeling and structure more than in doctrine.
- **Limits.** The shared web-search allowance ran out near the end. Anything I could not confirm is marked UNVERIFIED. This file runs longer than the brief's target because the assignment had twelve lines of inquiry; lesser sources are grouped.

## Strongest parallels

1. **The Seth material (Jane Roberts, 1963–84): each person makes their own copy of the world.** Steven: "There is no consensus mechanism. It is always your reality. You are the consensus mechanism"; when two people disagree about an apple, "They both happen" (06 @B0175). In *The Seth Material* (1970), Seth tells three people looking at one glass that each makes a separate one: "Each of the three of you creates your own glass, in your own personal perspective" (fan transcription, https://speakingofseth.com/index.php?msg=11539). This is the closest match to the multiplayer rules that I found. Steven recommends these books ("The Seth books are pretty good", 07 @B0046), so it is influence.

2. **The Hymn of the Pearl (Acts of Thomas): awake, see, remember.** Steven: "You need to see. You need to wake up. You need to remember" (04 @B0170). In the Hymn a letter reaches a king's son who has fallen asleep in Egypt and forgotten who he is. M. R. James's translation has "Rise up and awake out of sleep" and "remember that thou art a son of kings"; William Wright's has "Call to mind that thou art a son of kings! See the slavery,--whom thou servest!" All three of Steven's verbs are in the letter across the two standard translations. Neither Steven nor Joshua mentions the Hymn.

3. **Douglas Harding, *On Having No Head* (1961): a hole where the self was, filled with the world.** Steven: "You're the entire world with a boy shaped hole in the center" (04 @B0277). Harding describes "this nothing, this hole where a head should have been" and says "I had lost a head and gained a world" (https://headless.org/on-having-no-head). Both say it was obvious and simple. Harding claims no entities, powers or control.

4. **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.4.1–3: the one who finds only itself is afraid and joyless.** Steven: "I'm the only one here. I'm always the only one here. I'm just sock puppets with myself" (02 @B0182). The Upanishad's first Self looks around and "saw nothing else than the self"; then "He was afraid", "He, verily, had no delight", "He desired a second" (https://www.himalayanacademy.com/saivite-scriptures/?p=244). The emotional sequence is the same. The text is a creation myth, not a personal report.

5. **Vasubandhu and Leibniz: how many private worlds make a shared one.** Steven: "every individual soul has their own entire reality, and it is all made up of them" (06 @B0052); "The only way that anything could ever get into your reality is by suggestion" (06 @B0068). Vasubandhu (4th–5th c.) meets the same objection, why beings in one place see the same things, with shared karma and direct influence: "Minds affect minds directly" (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vasubandhu/). Leibniz's monads each mirror the whole universe and nothing passes between them. Leibniz needs God to keep the worlds in step; Steven's teacher says nothing does.

6. **Patanjali's samyama: becoming the object gives command of it.** Steven: "You basically just stare at something or a concept long enough that you realize that you are it" (06 @B0151). That is a fair plain account of *Yoga Sutras* 3.3–3.4, which Steven had read. Patanjali adds a warning Steven does not carry over: the powers "are obstacles to trance" (3.36 in Rama Prasada's numbering).

7. **The *Law of One* (Ra material, 1981–84): it is all you.** Steven: "It's all just you. You're always only interacting with yourself" (02 @B0173). Ra, session 1.7: "You are every thing, every being, every emotion, every event, every situation. You are unity. You are infinity" (https://www.lawofone.info/s/1). Named by Steven, so influence.

8. **Thomas Campbell, *My Big TOE* (2003): a rendered, multiplayer reality with a rule-set.** Steven: "It's just a different rule set" (05 @B0193); "It's just server settings" (06 @B0183). Campbell, a physicist from Robert Monroe's circle, describes physical reality as a virtual environment rendered to individual units of consciousness under a local "rule-set". He is not named in any chapter. The vocabulary is also what a software engineer who plays online games would reach for, so borrowing cannot be told from shared trade.

## Source notes

### roberts1970
- Citation: Jane Roberts, *The Seth Material* (Prentice-Hall, 1970); *The Nature of Personal Reality* (Prentice-Hall, 1974), notes by Robert F. Butts.
- Confirmed at: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Material ; glass passage at https://speakingofseth.com/index.php?msg=11539 (attributed there to *The Seth Material* ch. 10, session 68; not checked against print).
- What it reports: From late 1963 to 1984 Roberts spoke in trance as "Seth". Each person creates their own reality through thoughts, feelings and beliefs; the phrase "you create your own reality" may originate here. Physical objects are individual constructions. The 1974 book has a chapter on "natural hypnosis", in which beliefs work as suggestions the unconscious accepts (chapter 16 or 17 depending on the listing).
- Parallel: 06 @B0175; 06 @B0068; 02 @B0173. Closeness: close. Difference: Seth explains agreement between people by telepathy and mass belief; Steven's lecture explains stability by layers of self "loyal" to physics (06 @B0083) and denies any tiebreaker.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: yes (07 @B0046). Steven's blog outline "Reality 102: Souls", listed under 9 April 2026 and marked under construction, says in its first section: "Full stop (shoutout to Seth)" (https://differentcompass.ink/reality-102-souls).
- Standing and criticism: Channelled text; nothing establishes its source. Roberts herself called Seth's statements "theories". James Alcock saw no need for a supernatural agency; Paul Cunningham argued fraud and cryptomnesia are improbable. Papers at Yale. Regarded as a cornerstone of New Age thought.

### ra1984
- Citation: Don Elkins, Carla Rueckert, James Allen McCarty, *The Law of One* (the Ra Material), Books I–V, L/L Research; sessions 1981–84.
- Confirmed at: https://www.lawofone.info/s/1 ; https://www.lawofone.info/results.php?q=%22law+of+confusion%22
- What it reports: Channelled question-and-answer sessions teaching that all is one. Free will is the first "distortion", also called the Law of Confusion: "That is the Law of Confusion. You have called this the Law of Free Will" (3.14).
- Parallel: 02 @B0173. Closeness: close on the headline claim. Difference: Ra gives a cosmology of densities and polarity; Steven gives mechanics for one soul.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: yes (06 @B0176; 07 @B0046, where "Light and Love channeling books" is L/L Research). Note: Steven uses "the law of confusion" for the idea that "repelling something actually opens you up to it" (06 @B0176). In the passages I checked the term means free will.
- Standing and criticism: I found no independent scholarly assessment.

### campbell2003
- Citation: Thomas W. Campbell, *My Big TOE* (trilogy), Lightning Strike Books, 2003.
- Confirmed at: https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/thomas-warren-campbell/
- What it reports: Campbell (MS physics, Purdue, 1968; defence and NASA risk analysis) worked with Robert Monroe in the early 1970s. Consciousness is primary; the physical world is rendered as a game engine renders a game; a larger system runs virtual realities so individual consciousness can develop through free choices; many units share one environment under shared constraints. Secondary summaries give his terms "rule-set" and "multiplayer"; wording against the books UNVERIFIED.
- Parallel: 05 @B0193; 05 @B0252; 02 @B0203 "This is a multiplayer game"; 06 @B0147. Closeness: moderate (close in vocabulary). Difference: Campbell has one system computing one consistent reality, which is a consensus mechanism; Steven's teacher says "they have their own copy" (06 @B0061).
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no. Joshua names Monroe (06 @B0007).
- Standing and criticism: Self-published. The Psi Encyclopedia calls the analogy suggestive, not demonstrative. His proposed double-slit tests are unresolved; one version is reported falsified in an unpublished 2026 communication.

### hoffman2019
- Citation: Donald D. Hoffman, *The Case Against Reality*, W. W. Norton, 2019.
- Confirmed at: https://raintaxi.com/the-case-against-reality-why-evolution-hid-the-truth-from-our-eyes/
- What it reports: Perception is a species-specific interface tuned for fitness, not truth; objects in space-time are like desktop icons; underneath is a network of "conscious agents".
- Parallel: 02 @B0172 "I'm rendering my whole existence"; Joshua on Umwelt (06 @B0054). Closeness: moderate. Difference: Hoffman does not say objects are made of the perceiver or that belief alters physics.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no.
- Standing and criticism: A disputed research programme. Reviewers say the claim of no resemblance between interface and world outruns the evidence.

### kastrup2018
- Citation: Bernardo Kastrup, "The Universe in Consciousness", *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 25(5–6): 125–155, 2018.
- Confirmed at: https://philarchive.org/rec/KASTUI
- What it reports: One universal consciousness; living beings are its dissociated "alters", on the analogy of dissociative identity disorder.
- Parallel: 06 @B0070 "There are sequestered layers of self, and they have different allegiances to you and different roles"; Joshua's hand and fingers (06 @B0090). Closeness: moderate. Difference: Kastrup splits one mind into many subjects; Steven splits one soul into layers, most of them running physics.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no.
- Standing and criticism: Peer-reviewed philosophy. Philip Goff objects that dissociation cannot be assumed to yield a new subject (the "decombination problem").

### leibniz1714
- Citation: G. W. Leibniz, *Monadology* (1714), §§7, 56–57.
- Confirmed at: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Monadology_(Leibniz,_tr._Duncan) ; https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2007/entries/leibniz-causation/
- What it reports: Reality consists of simple mind-like substances. Nothing enters or leaves one. Each mirrors the whole universe from its own point of view, as one town looks different from different sides. Apparent interaction is a harmony God set up in advance.
- Parallel: 06 @B0052; 06 @B0061. Closeness: moderate. Difference: no influence at all between monads, and God coordinates; Steven allows suggestion with permission and has no coordinator.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no.
- Standing and criticism: Canonical metaphysics; no empirical standing.

### gold2021
- Citation: Jonathan C. Gold, "Vasubandhu", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2011; revised 2021), on the *Twenty Verses*.
- Confirmed at: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vasubandhu/
- What it reports: Vasubandhu defends "appearance only". Why do beings in one place and time experience the same objects? His example is hell, where the guards are apparitions produced by the shared karma of those they torment. Mental streams act on each other without physical go-betweens.
- Parallel: 06 @B0068; 06 @B0241 "we all agree on physics, right? And that's so we can coexist". Closeness: moderate to close in structure; the nearest classical answer to "how are there other people?" (02 @B0203). Difference: karma, not permission; the aim is release, not control.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no. Joshua says he studied Buddhism (02 @B0006).
- Standing and criticism: Philosophical argument, not evidence.

### lawrence_iep
- Citation: David Peter Lawrence, "Kashmiri Shaiva Philosophy", *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy*.
- Confirmed at: https://iep.utm.edu/kashmiri/
- What it reports: The Pratyabhijñā ("Recognition") school of Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta (10th–11th c.) holds that liberation is the self-recognition "I am Shiva", seen as the inner reality of all experience. "All sentient and insentient beings are essentially one awareness." Shakti is the capacity to emanate and control the universe.
- Parallel: 04 @B0246 "It feels like remembering, because it feels familiar"; 04 @B0274 "the hammock was no more or less me than the hand was"; 02 @B0187. Closeness: moderate to close: recognising what one already is, with power as a property of awareness. Difference: one Lord recognising himself, not a reality per soul.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no. Steven says "I think these are, like, Atman and Brahman experiences" (04 @B0233).
- Standing and criticism: Religious philosophy.

### brihadaranyaka
- Citation: Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.4.1–3, trans. S. Radhakrishnan (print details not verified).
- Confirmed at: https://www.himalayanacademy.com/saivite-scriptures/?p=244
- What it reports: In the beginning there was only the Self. It saw nothing but itself, was afraid, had no delight, desired a second and became two.
- Parallel: 02 @B0172 "And then I'm immediately disappointed at how simple it all is"; 02 @B0182. Closeness: close on the feeling. Difference: the Self ends its aloneness by dividing; Steven ends it by choosing: "I decide that it matters, just in that moment" (02 @B0176).

### harding1961
- Citation: D. E. Harding, *On Having No Head* (London: The Buddhist Society, 1961).
- Confirmed at: https://headless.org/on-having-no-head ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Harding
- What it reports: Looking for his own head in first-person experience, Harding found only the scene: "It was a vast emptiness vastly filled". It was like waking, and "perfectly simple and plain and straightforward".
- Parallel: 04 @B0267 "There is only a boy-shaped hole in my center"; 05 @B0079 "I could do my taxes. But I'm a hole in the center of my reality". Closeness: close (phenomenology). Difference: Steven adds that things are made of him and "I could feel that it loved me" (04 @B0275).
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no.
- Standing and criticism: A method of first-person "experiments"; I found no controlled research on it.

### james1902

### james1898 (with huxley1954, kelly2007, carhartharris2019): the filter theory
- Citation: William James, *Human Immortality* (1898). Aldous Huxley, *The Doors of Perception* (1954). E. F. Kelly et al., *Irreducible Mind* (Rowman & Littlefield, 2007); Kelly, Crabtree, Marshall (eds.), *Beyond Physicalism* (2015). R. L. Carhart-Harris and K. J. Friston, "REBUS and the Anarchic Brain", *Pharmacological Reviews* 71(3): 316–344, 2019.
- Confirmed at: https://www.religion-online.org/book/human-immortality/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_at_Large ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_Mind ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6588209
- What it reports: James argued the brain may transmit or permit consciousness, as a prism shapes light, and not produce it. Huxley called the brain a "reducing valve" on "Mind at Large". Kelly's group revived the view from F. W. H. Myers. The nearest mainstream model, REBUS, says psychedelics relax the brain's high-level expectations so more raw signal gets through; it claims nothing about mind beyond the brain.
- Parallel: 02 @B0094 "We have filters up"; Joshua's "apertures" (06 @B0134); Steven's ray of light through prisms (04 @B0233) is James's own image. Closeness: moderate. Difference: in the filter theory the brain narrows a wider mind; for Steven the narrowing is done by layers of oneself.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no.
- Standing and criticism: A minority position. *Irreducible Mind* had a positive review in *Journal of Mind and Behavior* and negative ones in *American Journal of Psychology* and *Skeptic*. A 2023 paper finds Huxley altered the passage he quoted from C. D. Broad.

### josipovic2014 (with martin2020, metzinger2024): research on non-dual awareness
- Citation: Z. Josipovic, *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 1307: 9–18, 2014; Josipovic et al., *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 5:183, 2012. J. A. Martin, *CONSCIOUSNESS: Ideas and Research for the Twenty-First Century* 8(8), 2020. T. Metzinger, *The Elephant and the Blind* (MIT Press, 2024).
- Confirmed at: https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2011.00183/full ; https://digitalcommons.ciis.edu/conscjournal/vol8/iss8/1/ ; https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780262547109
- What it reports: In one fMRI study of meditators, the usual opposition between the brain's outward-facing and inward-facing networks weakened during non-dual awareness practice. Martin interviewed people reporting lasting shifts: the sense of self first feels expanded and connected, thoughts lose their grip, and people go on functioning. Metzinger analyses hundreds of meditators' reports of "pure awareness" from 57 countries.
- Parallel: 05 @B0101 "My thoughts and emotions are more co-equal with my surroundings"; 05 @B0079; 04 @B0277 "I can now reclaim that state on purpose". Closeness: moderate to close as a description of the state. Difference: none treats the state as showing that objects are made of the meditator.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no.
- Standing and criticism: The brain finding is one study. Martin's paper is qualitative, in a non-mainstream journal, with a mostly white male American sample, and he runs a paid course on it. Metzinger stresses that agreement among reports is not evidence about reality.

### hymnpearl
- Citation: "Hymn of the Soul" (Hymn of the Pearl), Acts of Thomas 108–113. Trans. M. R. James, *The Apocryphal New Testament* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1924); trans. William Wright, *Apocryphal Acts of the Apostles* (London, 1871), pp. 238–245.

### sheldrake1981 (with merton1961, gilbert2015, akashic): the shared library
- Citation: Rupert Sheldrake, *A New Science of Life* (1981). Robert K. Merton, "Singletons and Multiples in Scientific Discovery", *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society* 105, 1961. Elizabeth Gilbert, *Big Magic* (2015). "Akashic records" (Theosophy: Sinnett 1883, Leadbeater 1899).
- Confirmed at: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maddox ; https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse548/19au/papers/merton.pdf ; https://chapter16.org/the-way-and-the-truth-of-creative-living/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akashic_records
- What it reports: Sheldrake proposed that once something has been done or learned, a "morphic resonance" makes it easier everywhere (as usually summarised; I confirmed the book and its reception, not this wording). Merton held that independent multiple discovery is the common pattern in science and tied it to the shared state of knowledge. Gilbert tells of an abandoned Amazon novel that Ann Patchett then wrote; Patchett thought it a generic idea. The Akashic records are a Theosophical cosmic record read by clairvoyants.
- Parallel: 02 @B0198 "Everything that has ever been created goes into this library, like a blueprint library"; 05 @B0206 "every time somebody does something like this, I think it's easier for everyone else to do it". Closeness: close for Sheldrake on "easier for everyone"; moderate for the Akashic records, which record events; loose for Plato's Forms, which are never created.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: Joshua names the Akashic Record (02 @B0212), *Big Magic* (02 @B0235) and Darwin and Wallace (02 @B0244).
- Standing and criticism: John Maddox's 1981 *Nature* editorial called Sheldrake's book pseudo-science; I found no accepted replication. Merton supplies the ordinary explanation of simultaneous ideas.

### proietti2019 (with bong2020, fuchs2014): the physics sometimes cited
- Citation: M. Proietti et al., *Science Advances* 5(9): eaaw9832, 2019. K.-W. Bong et al., *Nature Physics* 16: 1199–1205, 2020. C. A. Fuchs, N. D. Mermin, R. Schack, *American Journal of Physics* 82: 749–754, 2014.
- Confirmed at: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754223 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05607 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.5253
- What it reports: Both experiments build an extended Wigner's-friend set-up from photons and violate an inequality. Proietti: "at least one of the three assumptions of free choice, locality, and observer-independent facts must fail". Bong: "a proof-of-principle experiment where a photon's path is deemed an observer". QBism reads quantum theory as one agent's tool for organising their own experience.
- Parallel: 06 @B0175. Closeness: loose. The results say which assumptions about measurement records cannot all hold. The "observers" were photons. Nothing here shows that people live in separate realities or that belief changes outcomes; dropping locality or free choice keeps facts observer-independent.
- Named by Steven or Joshua: no. Steven mentions "the Everettian interpretation" in passing (06 @B0222).
- Standing and criticism: Sound experiments, open interpretation, acknowledged loopholes. Okon (2022) argues the theorem constrains reality little.

### basmajian1963 (with dworkin1986, ehrsson2007): awareness before control; the rubber hand
- Citation: J. V. Basmajian, *Science* 141: 440–441, 1963. B. R. Dworkin and N. E. Miller, *Behavioral Neuroscience* 100(3): 299–314, 1986. H. H. Ehrsson et al., *PNAS* 104(23): 9828–9833, 2007.

## What is distinctive in Steven's account

- **"There is no consensus mechanism."** Every many-minds idealism I checked supplies a coordinator: God (Leibniz), karma (Vasubandhu), a larger system (Campbell), telepathic mass agreement (Seth), one underlying mind (Kastrup, Ra). Steven's teacher refuses one. The sequel, that the other person stays in your world as something like "a fingernail" with the lights off (06 @B0220–@B0222), I found nowhere else. Steven holds it "with much lower confidence" (06 @B0225) and says "I don't like it" (06 @B0229).
- **Layers of self with allegiances.** Dissociated parts are common in the literature. Parts of yourself "loyal to running the software" of physics, the "operating system" under the game character (06 @B0075, @B0083), are his own. This is how the model leaves ordinary physics and medicine standing (06 @B0111).
- **Suggestion as the only way in, and closing in order to create.** Being closed is the blank canvas that makes original work possible (06 @B0165). I found no parallel.
- **The library is written, not eternal.** "we can only create things that exist" (02 @B0202). Reality is assembled from blueprints someone once made, which makes creative work cumulative and sacred (02 @B0225–@B0232).
- **The emotional shape.** Disappointment, loneliness, then a decision ("I decided that it's real", 02 @B0176), and a felt "collective exhale" when he decides again awake (04 @B0271). The Upanishad and Zen come near; the let-down followed by a chosen yes is unusual.
- **Reciprocity from objects.** "it knew and I knew that we were the same thing again" (04 @B0276). Unity is common in such reports; being loved back by a bowl is not.
- **Power gated by responsibility as a safety design** (06 @B0141–@B0148), with the baby-gate image.
- **The form.** A Socratic lecture with props (frog, rat, two people), numbered like a course catalogue (06 @B0047), with the teacher's own caveat that it is "correct, it's just not complete" (06 @B0064).

## What a careful skeptic would say

1. **The sources were available, and some were read.** Steven recommends Seth and the *Law of One*, had read the *Yoga Sutras*, and uses *The Matrix* throughout. His blog outline of the second lecture, listed under 9 April 2026, credits Seth (the page may have been edited later). A half-asleep mind can assemble a lecture from what it holds, and this lecture speaks his trade's language. He says of the first experience "Coming into this, I had not done a lot of study on esoteric stuff" (02 @B0082), so the order of reading and experience matters and is not settled.
2. **Practised inner attention makes presences real.** Luhrmann's work predicts the path Steven describes: months of treating faint inner impressions as persons, among people who hold minds to be porous, yields vivid, responsive others. Steven also wrote fantasy and horror (01 @B0004), and nearly all fiction writers in Taylor's study met characters with minds of their own. This explains the experience without calling it illness; Powers and colleagues show such voice-hearing in healthy people.
3. **The states are real; the inference is the question.** Non-dual awareness is a documented, trainable state. Moving from "everything feels like me" to "everything is made of me" is the step James said carries authority only for the experiencer. The same loss of boundaries is read as insight in one setting and as depersonalization in another (Lindahl and Britton 2019). Relaxed high-level expectations (REBUS, proposed for psychedelics; applied here by analogy) would produce both the boundary loss and the certainty.
4. **The model is hard to test.** Rules are soft; other people act "like dampeners" (05 @B0200); doubt "kills these experiences" (02 @B0090); in a conflict "They both happen" (06 @B0175). Each protects the model from a failed demonstration. In fairness Steven asks for more ("I insisted on validation", 02 @B0263) and marks what was shown and what he inferred.
5. **The physics does not help.** The Wigner's-friend experiments used photons as observers and show only that a set of assumptions cannot all hold.
6. **A moral hazard.** "The reason that anything happens to you is because you allow it" (06 @B0116) can turn into blaming the unfortunate. Steven rejects that reading (06 @B0117), but the sentence will be quoted without his caveat.
