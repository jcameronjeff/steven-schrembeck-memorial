subtitle: A conversation with Dr. Christopher Loo
source: n1300

[note] Steven was a guest on the *Financial Freedom Podcast*, hosted by Dr. Christopher H. Loo, who introduced him as a startup founder from Georgia, just outside Atlanta, working on AI for self-control and stability. What follows is a selection from their conversation.

## A hazmat suit

@B0017 Chris
You talk about developing AI tools to help individuals regain control over their attention and choices in a increasingly distracted world. Elaborate on that and what that means.

@B0018 Steven
A lot of these general concepts you can start to apply in your own life. You don't need to use anything that I'm making or anything else. But think of it this way. Large language models specifically, and all the stuff that gets bolted on top of them, I'll just shorthand it to large language models. There's a lot of magic under the covers. It's the same with Google. You type in a search box, but you just know that there's an ocean of code under there. So there's a lot of stuff that goes into this, but for now assume it's large language models.

@B0019 Steven
It's basically commodity intelligence. You can think of it as not like an especially bright person, just like your average person, an average level of reasoning in a box. A general reasoning, and that is really powerful. One thing you can use that for, yes, you can use it to replace people. However you feel about that, good or bad, is neutral. But you can use it to replace commodity intelligence. Everyone tends to focus on that.

@B0020 Steven
But you could also use it in places where it would be unethical, unreasonable, or just not preferable to have a human do the same thing. So think of it like a hazmat suit, or if you've seen the show *Chernobyl* or whatever, where they try to get the robots to go in and clean it up. So you put it in environments where you wouldn't want a human. So you can imagine there's a lot of environments like that on the internet. It's a lot more dangerous, predatory. There's a lot of influences.

@B0021 Steven
So some simple ways to use it are just send an AI ahead of you just to go look for things that it's triggering or is it good, and just pacify it or sanitize some of the things, or even just censor some of the things you don't want to experience. Get rid of the noise, the intentional bias and influences, all that sort of stuff. So you can think of this as having a hazmat suit.

@B0022 Steven
So that is a simplistic way of looking at that, but there's a lot of other environments and situations in which you would like to have human-like intelligence evaluate a situation without a human so that a human doesn't have to. So not just saving work, it's also sparing a conscious creature from having to experience or go through this.

## The untrustworthy operator

@B0028 Chris
You talk about the AI tools assisting those struggling with ADHD or addiction. How is this possible? What are specific features designed to help those struggling with ADHD and addiction?

@B0029 Steven
There's lots of different hows. It's like a constellation of different tools, and a lot of it's just regular software. But one of the fundamental problems is you have the untrustworthy operator problem. Let's say you've got a bunch of system that keeps you healthy, in check. "I meditate at this time. I take my medication at this time. I don't go buy drugs or alcohol 'cause I'm not doing that. I make sure that I check into work, or I leave by this time." You can imagine a whole bunch of systems that people with ADHD and autism rely on.

@B0030 Steven
However, the operator themselves is not trustworthy, both in the sense that they forget, they have low object permanence, they just don't even realize that they should be doing this, or they have emotional irregularity or motivational instability. So you simply can't operate in a way that's... Okay, "I have a personal rule not to go buy alcohol on the way home." Okay, that's not gonna stop you. The operator themselves can't be trusted.

@B0031 Steven
So now what do you do when the system you rely on, you can't trust the person who's operating it? And you can have a nanny, or you can have, I guess, a babysitter. You could have a sponsor. There are piecemeal solutions to all this stuff. But what would be really nice is if you had a system of accountability that understood the nuance. There's that commodified intelligence. This is where the AI part comes in. "What state are you in? Can I trust you? Yes or no?"

@B0032 Steven
Normally you can change your rules. It's just a rules engine. "I do this, I don't do this, make it hard to do this, hide this from me." It knows you can't be trusted, so there's this balance. This is where you need the intelligence. "Do I let you just turn all this stuff off? Do I try to block a transaction at a liquor store?" "Okay, I'm just buying this in order to make a flambé later." There's a lot of nuance here that brute force, simplistic tools don't work.

@B0033 Steven
So you need something that understands. Also, you don't want it interrupting you or stopping you or blocking your websites when you're trying to do stuff for work. There's an enormous amount of nuance in when to apply enforcement, and the net result of all this is that it just makes it easy to do what you wanna do, harder to do what you don't wanna do, and it makes it more or less impossible to not realize what you should be doing. And then the rest is more or less just set up your own rules.

@B0034 Steven
So that's the general system. It's for sleep, it's for electronic addictions, avoiding substance abuse, spending control. These are the most common problems that people have, and so that's what we're focused on. But the principles are general. It's whatever rules you got for life. But just imagine a little guardian angel that is constantly paying attention to what you're doing. There's some AI there as well. And then whatever information you choose to give it, it's using that to evaluate whether it should step in to enforce your own rules or not if you don't.

## A digital adult in the room

@B0035 Chris
How do the AI tools you're developing help users attain mental clarity, and can you share examples of how these tools are used?

@B0036 Steven
I'm operating from the state that your standard human at rest is already healthy, so if that's not true, that's a different target audience. So assuming that at rest when healthy you're functional, then it is mostly about removing influences. It's about decreasing your level of stimulation to improve your baseline dopamine. So a lot of this is like the hazmat suit I mentioned. It's creating different interfaces to influences. Okay, you wanna listen to podcasts? You go through this other tool that desanitizes it. You wanna browse the web, you want to look at social media, you're gonna do it through this alternate interface that doesn't allow these things to pull your attention away.

@B0037 Steven
You can also tell it what your intentions are. This is what the first version of the software was. Tell it what your intentions are, and then it's paying attention to what you're doing, and if there's a discrepancy, it can just make you aware. You're doing this to yourself. If you choose to follow up by adding more restrictions, "Hey, I said I was doing this thing for work and I'm not doing it after 45 minutes," go through this pretty well-known psychotherapy routine, mirroring.

@B0038 Steven
None of this is new. I'm just putting together existing Legos. "Follow this step, then this step. If I completely ignore it, then I want you to turn off my internet." Not everyone's gonna do this kind of thing, but for certain problems it makes sense. Like substance abuse, for instance. You need a hard guardrail which says, "If a transaction comes through or is trying to be processed from these merchants or in this way, deny it." And that's just a hard enforcement.

@B0039 Steven
It's never gonna be able to stop you. The purpose is just to add friction or to remove the presence of things that are making your life worse so that you don't have to use willpower. The whole purpose is you cannot rely on willpower. These people specifically, I'm definitely in the target audience, cannot rely on willpower reliably.

@B0039 Steven
So it adds a backstop, and ultimately allows you to retrain your brain for what you want and what you pay attention to because there is a digital adult in the room that will eventually step in, and that is something that we lose when neurodiverse people come from childhood to adulthood. There's nobody to step in anymore if you live alone or you're supporting yourself or others are dependent on you. You need reliable ways to not crash and burn. There's no one to step in.

@B0040 Steven
And so now this sort of preserves autonomy and independence and allows some software to step in by your own design. You're doing this to yourself. It's configurable. So that's the premise.

## People know what to do

@B0041 Chris
What are your thoughts on society's current challenges with self-control, and how can technology, particularly AI, foster more mindful and intentional behaviors?

@B0042 Steven
Anyone relatively studied in psychology or sociology recognizes just how we like to think we have free will, and like we have a lot of agency. But most of our behaviors and the ways in which we can be influenced are very well documented. They're super well-documented blueprints on how to get humans to do what you want them to do. And the sad part of this is, that has been very well perfected and engineered upon by basically everything. Everything from business processes to the well-known attention and influence economies, ads, all this sort of stuff. It's obvious people have figured out really how to perfectly manipulate others through technology.

@B0043 Steven
The nice part is that you can do that to yourself, and you can also detect that, and you can stop it. So that is the role of software in this, is that you can use software to harm yourself or to be harmed, and you can just accept that. Or at the individual level or an organizational level, you don't have to wait for society to change. You can just make your own tools or set your own rules. None of this has to be software. But what to access and what not to access, and become aware of what's manipulating you or influencing you.

@B0044 Steven
And so that is really how I see it, is yes, collective action is great. The man is slow, frustrating, and often ineffective. Solutions are not great. So if you're waiting for US Congress to pass laws banning social media so that your 12-year-old isn't being unduly influenced, I think you're gonna be waiting a long time and you're gonna be disappointed.

@B0045 Steven
However, you can begin to take action now. A lot of it's simple, and a lot of it is the willingness to notice how things are making you feel. That is such a simple thing to say, but notice what is making you feel bad, become committed to that, and then be willing to live differently or to be creative on how you remove or blunt those influences on you. That's the most important thing to do regardless of what tools you use, 'cause nothing is ever perfect. You gotta fit it to your life. Remove the bad stuff and do more of the good stuff.

@B0046 Steven
I don't think that any of this is actually surprising. My insight for building a business in software was that people know what to do. The average person knows what is healthy for them and not healthy for them generally, but they have trouble actually doing it or not doing it. So that's where you should focus. Less on shame. Stop using effort and willpower, and just make it so that it's really hard, if not impossible, to do the things that are bad for you, and it's really easy, if not inevitable, to do the things that are good for you.

## What are you replacing

@B0053 Chris
With AI, how do you help people use technology to create more meaningful lives? Ultimately, with technology, we're losing meaning. We're very connected, but we're some of the most disconnected people in human history, even though the technology is supposed to bring us together. So how do you use AI to create more meaning for people?

@B0054 Steven
That's a great question, and I'm willing to be late for my next thing to answer that because that's an amazing question. And I feel very strongly about this. We should not be replacing the behaviors that we want to do. This is where capitalist profiteering, like efficiency mindset, falls flat. If we keep going, we've automated away everything. We have AI raising our children. We have technology that does everything for us. Eventually, it can just live for us, and what are we? You can just be put on a feeding tube and just go unconscious.

@B0055 Steven
What are you replacing? I think this is where we have to be quite mindful. At an individual or even an organizational level, what are you omitting? What are you actually replacing? Did you get rid of the things that you wanted to do? The purpose of an AI tool is the obvious that most people talk about, to replace the jobs and tasks that people don't want to do, or that it can do it better. Sure, that's fine. Go use it for those things. That's obvious.

@B0056 Steven
Pay attention to where it's overrunning and recognize that there is meaning in work, there's meaning in helping people. What are you doing with this extra time? Unfortunately, if the common denominator, the status quo, is that you go back to the attention economy, you start grazing for information, or you're in a world where nobody else is around, in the physical world it's very lonely because they're all zombies, that's bad.

@B0057 Steven
What are you freeing up your time and energy for? So you want time, energy, and attention freedom. That's awesome. You can keep opening that up, but if the way you allocate it is just pointless leisure or it has no meaning for you, then you're just getting more of a terrible thing.

@B0057 Steven
So this is, again, a neutral tool. All it does is give you more time, energy, and attention freedom, maybe some financial freedom as well. But what are you gonna do with that? You have an excess, you have a surplus of these things, but how you allocate that for meaning is critical. It creates more of a thing, but it doesn't mean that you're gonna use your newfound freedom and resources. That is an entirely different conversation and problem. I have a lot to say on those, but I think we'll have to leave it at that for now.
