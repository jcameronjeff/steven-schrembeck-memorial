Steven called his story a template. This chapter tries to draw the template out: the stations his account passes through, who else has reported each one, and what has and hasn't been checked. I made it for two kinds of reader. One is the person this is happening to, who wants to know whether anyone else has stood where they are standing. The other is the person who, like me, wants to know how much weight the whole thing will bear.

Two rules first, because a map like this is easy to misuse.

Agreement among reports is not evidence about reality. A philosopher who has studied hundreds of meditators' accounts of pure awareness makes exactly that point about his own data {cite:metzinger2024}. People can agree because they met the same thing. They can also agree because they have the same kind of brain, or read the same books.

So for every comparison I have tried to say which of three kinds it is. A lineage is something he learned from a teacher or a book, and it tells you what he had read. An independent match is someone far away in time or place, whom he shows no sign of knowing, saying the same thing. And some things, as far as I can find, are only his.

## 1. The crack

A careful worldview fails at one point, and the person is honest enough to let the failure spread. {q:01-prologue|What else am I wrong about, right?}

This is common enough among scientists and engineers to have been given a name, the flip {cite:kripal2019}. The designer of the first commercial microprocessor tells a version of it {cite:faggin2021}. For many people, as for Steven, it starts with something seen in the sky {cite:mack1994,ring1992}.

Checked: there is nothing here to test. It is a pattern in biographies, and it is independent of him.

## 2. The loosening

Practice wears the edges thin. For him it was years of daily meditation, and then the states on either side of sleep. {q:07-telepathy|All the filters come down}, he says of the moment before a nap.

Long, still, eyes-closed practice reliably produces lights, heat, visions and a changed sense of self {cite:lindahl2017}. The border of sleep has its own literature of autonomous images and voices {cite:mavromatis1987}. William James proposed in 1898 that the brain may limit consciousness more than it produces it, and used the image of a prism, which is the image Steven is shown in February {cite:james1898}. The nearest mainstream model says psychedelics work by relaxing the brain's high-level expectations so that more gets through, and claims nothing about a mind beyond the brain {cite:carhartharris2019}.

Checked: the states are real, they can be trained, and they have been measured. What comes through them is the question.

## 3. The others

A presence becomes a person. Communication comes as feeling and intent first and words second, and it comes through your own mind. {q:07-telepathy|they're using your hardware and your reality.}

His vocabulary here is a lineage that runs back through his teachers to Robert Monroe {cite:monroe1985}. But the mechanics are described by people well outside it: Swedenborg in 1758, who says spirits can only speak through a person's own language and memory {cite:swedenborg1758}, and Warcollier in the 1920s, who found that what arrives is reshaped by the receiver {cite:warcollier1948}.

The ordinary reading is strong at this station. Novelists' characters talk back {cite:taylor2003}. Churchgoers learn to hear God by practicing {cite:luhrmann2012}. And there are healthy people who hear kind voices every day and can turn them off {cite:powers2017}.

Checked: the experience is documented in people who are not ill. Laboratory telepathy shows a small effect in its supporters' hands and none in its critics' {cite:storm2010,miltonwiseman1999}.

## 4. The family

You were never alone. Someone has been there the whole time, and they love you. {q:03-see-wake-up-remember-1|you are loved, and you're not alone, and you never were}

The companion who is your own double, and who brings the teaching, is an old idea. The prophet Mani said his heavenly twin came to him at twelve and again at twenty-four {cite:stang2016}. Modern hypnosis subjects describe soul groups, guides and classrooms {cite:newton2000}; that material was widely read in the circles Steven moved in, so I count it as possible lineage and not as confirmation.

His: a mother of souls who is not God, who asked for a child and was surprised to get twins. A sister who bakes a cake and tells him his slice is too big. I found nothing this domestic anywhere.

Checked: nothing here can be.

## 5. The lesson

It is all you. {q:02-reality-101|It's all just you. You're always only interacting with yourself.}

As doctrine, he had read this in Seth and the *Law of One* {cite:roberts1970,ra1984}. As experience, the independent matches are good. Douglas Harding found a hole where his head should have been, filled with the world {cite:harding1961}. A medieval school in Kashmir taught that freedom is recognizing what you already are {cite:lawrence_iep}. An Upanishad describes a Self that discovers it is alone and is afraid {cite:brihadaranyaka}. And the instruction that led him there, to see, wake up and remember, is the content of a letter in a poem from the early centuries of Christianity {cite:hymnpearl_james1924}.

His: the disappointment, and then the decision. {q:02-reality-101|I decide that it matters, just in that moment.} Most reports of this state are about bliss.

Checked: the state is real. That things are made of you is an inference from it, and nobody has found a way to test the inference.

## 6. The rules for more than one

Each soul has its own whole reality. Things enter it by permission. When two people want different things, nothing arbitrates. {q:06-reality-102|every individual soul has their own entire reality, and it is all made up of them}

The problem is ancient and so are the attempts on it {cite:leibniz1714,gold2021}. The nearest modern statement is Seth's, which he knew and credited {cite:schrembeck2026}.

His: the refusal of any coordinator, and the layers of self that stay loyal to physics. Also his own doubt about the last part of it.

Checked: nothing. The physics sometimes cited for it does not show it {cite:proietti2019}.

## 7. The proof

He asked for validation and says he got it: a lost thing found, a wheel that turned, cards read with his eyes shut, a fork tied in a knot. {q:00-invitation|And if it's true, it should be verifiable.}

This is the station where he staked the most and where the outside record is weakest. Each of his abilities has a history of other people reporting it, some of them thousands of people {cite:houck1983,romains1920}. None has been demonstrated under conditions that rule out the ordinary explanation, and where the conditions were tightened the effect went away {cite:gardner1966,pamplincollins1975}. The government-funded research on remote viewing ended in 1995 with two reviewers agreeing that the laboratory numbers were not chance and disagreeing about what that meant {cite:utts1995,hyman1996}. His own claims were never tested, which is different from tested and failed.

Checked: not his. Similar claims, with null or disputed results.

## 8. The return

What opens all of it, he says, is becoming a better person, and what you do with it is help. {q:01-prologue|Working through your problems is what gives you psychic powers.}

Nearly every tradition that describes these states says something like this, and the oldest manual he read ties powers to conduct {cite:patanjali}.

Checked: by a life. He seems to have lived it.

## What the map shows

Three things, I think.

First, the experience is real, common, and has a shape. Each station on his route has been reported by other people, some of them centuries apart with no way of copying each other. If this is happening to you, you are not the first and it is not, by itself, an illness. His psychiatrist told him the same.

Second, a good deal of the doctrine reached him through a lineage: his teachers, the Seth books, the *Law of One*, the *Yoga Sutras*. The independent matches are mostly in the shape and the feeling: the forgetting and remembering, the hole at the center, the loneliness, the voice that has to use your own words. I find those harder to explain away than the doctrine, and they are also the parts that make no claims about physics.

Third, the claims that could be checked from outside have not been. That is the gap between this book and what he wanted it to be, and it can be closed.

## If you are in the middle of this

I am not qualified to advise anyone, so I will repeat what he did.

He got himself assessed. If you are frightened, or can't sleep, or can't do your job, see someone, and know that there are clinicians who understand that a spiritual crisis is not automatically a psychiatric one {cite:grof1989,lukoff1992}. There are organizations for people going through this and for meditators in difficulty {cite:aciste,cheetahhouse,spiritualcrisis}.

He kept his life. He went to work, raised his children, and says plainly that if you aren't in a stable place you should wait.

He kept control. {q:07-telepathy|You are sovereign.} He says to hang up on anything that feels wrong, and to stop altogether if you are scared.

He found people to talk to. And he held it the way he held everything: {q:03-see-wake-up-remember-1|You can't rubber band this hard or you'll kill it. You'll crush it. You also can't become so obsessive that you become delusional}.

## What I think

This part is speculation, and it is where I am today, not where I expect to stay.

There are three stories that fit what is on these pages.

In the first, an unusually absorbed and inventive mind, trained by years of meditation and by a community with a shared vocabulary, did what such minds do. It made company for itself and built a cosmology out of what it had read, in an engineer's idiom. Nothing in this story makes him dishonest or unwell. All of the experiences are real. No one else is there.

In the second, what he said is what happened.

In the third, something real is reached, and it can only arrive through the mind of the person reaching, so it comes out sounding like that person's reading and that person's trade. This is, as it happens, exactly what his own model predicts, and it is the story I am most drawn to. I am also aware that it is the most comfortable of the three, and that as stated it can't be proved wrong.

So the useful question is where the first story and the second predict different things. I can find three places.

Information he could not have had. He says he was told where lost things were. He says a drawing of sunspots matched the sun that day; a dated drawing can be compared with the solar record. Someone sent him a message about grandmothers the morning after a sitting; she can be asked what she knew.

The abilities, under conditions agreed in advance.

And the order of things. If the journals show the experiences coming before the books, the first story has more to explain. If the books came first, the second does.

None of those is out of reach. He said of himself: {q:08-transcendence|There's nothing fundamentally different about me compared to you.} If that is true, the sample does not have to stay at one.
