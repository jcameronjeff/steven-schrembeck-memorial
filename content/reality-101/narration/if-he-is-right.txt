I said in the preface that my third reason was selfish. This is that chapter.

My friend is dead. He spent his last year telling anyone who would listen that nobody dies, and he was not being poetic. He meant that the people he had come to know are {q:02-reality-101|just people}, that when you die {q:05-see-wake-up-remember-3|you get to keep your memories, your personal development, your life lessons}, and that the distance between here and wherever they are can be talked across by anyone willing to practice.

If he was right, the sensible thing to do is what he said to do. If he was wrong, I will have spent some evenings thinking hard about my friend, which I was going to do anyway.

## He left instructions

I don't have to guess what he would want me to try. In the telepathy journal, when he needs an example of someone you might want to call, this is the one he reaches for:

> [07-telepathy] Let's say I wanted to talk to a deceased loved one. Say I wanted to talk to my grandmother, okay?

And he ends two of his last three recordings the same way. The telepathy journal:

> [07-telepathy] And, I don't know, give me a shout one day. Maybe I'll hear you. But be nice, like I'm not gonna take your phone call if you're gonna be rude to me. Assuming I can receive it. You never know.

The transcendence journal:

> [08-transcendence] And with that, send me a message with your mind one day. Be polite. Maybe I'll say hey if I know how to do it, if I get it.

I don't know when he recorded those, or what he had in mind. I know that he said it twice.

## The procedure, in his words

This is his method as he gives it in {ch:07-telepathy}, in his order.

Find the feeling of the person. He calls it a soul tone and says it works like a phone number.

> [07-telepathy] If you think about somebody a lot and think about an overarching feeling of what it's like when you think about them, you are creating a sort of shorthand that I call a soul tone, and this is basically your phone number.

Ask.

> [07-telepathy] "Hey, can we speak? May we speak?" It's always good to ask.

Then stop trying.

> [07-telepathy] All you really have to do is relax, open your mind, and allow thoughts to happen. You don't make it happen.

Use the edge of sleep.

> [07-telepathy] If you can almost fall asleep, like taking a nap, that is when you get the clearest communication because the parts of your brain that are used to policing self and doing all that, they just go.

Assume he hears you better than you hear him.

> [07-telepathy] So you should assume that most of the time they're getting your messages correctly, or clearly, I won't say correctly, to a far greater degree than you are receiving their messages. So talking is easier than listening.

Assume most of what comes back is you.

> [07-telepathy] But just assume that seventy to ninety percent of the information is just you talking to yourself, even once you've learned how to establish communication with somebody who's definitely trying.

Check what you feel in your chest against what you think you heard.

> [07-telepathy] Whatever your somatic sense is, they end up being a good error-checking device.

Ask for things you can check, and expect partial credit.

> [07-telepathy] So there are ways to validate, but I would suggest getting something that you can get partial credit on.

Take it seriously and hold it lightly.

> [07-telepathy] There's a balance to treating it as if this is real, and that's a person, and that's definitely happening right now. That should be your default state, but also don't over-index on it.

And stop if it frightens you.

> [07-telepathy] But if you're really scared or you don't like this, don't do it or stop doing it. Just remember that you have the control over it.

That is the whole method. It needs no equipment and nobody's money.

## What I notice about it

Three things, reading it as an engineer.

The channel is lopsided, and he says so up front. Sending works far better than receiving. So the one part I can do with no skill at all, which is to talk to him, is by his account the part most likely to arrive. I find I am willing to act on that.

He builds in the null result. By his own numbers, seven to nine tenths of anything I think I hear back is me. He is not asking me to trust a warm thought in his voice. He is telling me that it is probably mine, and to keep going anyway and see what is left over.

And he points at the test. In the Prologue he says he asked for validation and got it in small, checkable ways, such as where a lost thing was:

> [01-prologue] I go look in the bottom drawer near the sink, and there it is, and there's just this sense of laughter.

That is the difference between consolation and evidence, and he knew it. Ask for something you don't know and can check. Write it down before you look. Count the misses.

## What he wanted from his own dead

One more thing in his account matters here, and it is not about method.

He tried this himself, with mixed results. In the first lecture his twin starts to bring in his grandmother, who had died, and he refuses:

> [02-reality-101] For whatever reason, deceased family members is a line for me.

Later he sits with five mediums in training. Nothing they say is a clear hit, and he comes away sad, and works out why:

> [01-prologue] Okay, cool, but that's all I really wanted was that you think about me and you care about me. I didn't know to ask for that.

The next morning someone from the same training texts him, out of the blue, that she had a strange feeling she needed to tell him something about grandmas and loving him. You can make of that what you like. He does: {q:01-prologue|if you buy the story, I mean, that happened}.

What I take from it is simple. When Steven was the one on this side, a message with advice in it left him cold. What he wanted to hear from the people he had lost was that they thought about him and cared about him. So that is where I'll start.

He also knew this from the other side. Not long after the first lecture, on the night before his birthday, he heard his father, who had died some years before, say three words in a dream: {q:04-see-wake-up-remember-2|I know that it wasn't a dream. It was him.}

## What other people report

I expected the research on this to be thin. It isn't. The thin part is the part I most wanted.

Sensing the dead is common. In the first careful study, a Welsh doctor interviewed 293 widows and widowers and found that nearly four in ten had felt the presence of the husband or wife who had died. The experiences often went on for years, and most people found them a help {cite:rees1971}. A recent review of the research concludes that the great majority of such experiences are benign, and advises clinicians to welcome hearing about them {cite:kamp2020}. In a national survey in 2023, about a third of American adults said they had felt the presence of a dead relative, and more than a quarter had told a dead relative about their life within the past year {cite:pew2023}. Since the 1990s grief researchers have treated a continuing relationship with the dead as normal and not as a failure to let go {cite:klass1996}.

It happens where he said to look. In a survey of nearly a thousand people who reported contact with someone who had died, sleep and the edges of sleep were the most common setting {cite:elsaesser2021}, and in a hospice study more than half of the bereaved had dreamed of the person {cite:wright2014}.

And what people report hearing is what he wanted to hear. The researchers sort the messages into reassuring, resolving, reaffirming and releasing, and sum up one kind as I love you, I will always be by your side, we will meet again {cite:elsaesser2021}.

His sign-off has company too. In a Dutch hospital study of 344 people who survived cardiac arrest, 62 reported a near-death experience. Years later those 62 feared death less and believed in an afterlife more than the survivors who had no such experience {cite:vanlommel2001}. That is his "you're not going to die," arrived at by another road. I can't call it independent. Accounts like these have been everywhere since the 1970s.

## What this can and cannot show

Every one of those studies establishes that people have the experience. None establishes where it comes from, and the people who wrote them say so.

From the inside there is no mark on a thought that says who sent it. Steven says this himself. He describes how a message from someone you love gets rewritten by your own fear before you hear it, and how the rewritten line {q:07-telepathy|feels exactly like the line before it}. His checks, asking again and reading the feeling in his chest, use the same mind that may be making the answer.

Where the outside world has been allowed to keep score, the record is discouraging. In two large hospital studies, hidden images were placed where only a person looking down from above could see them; no patient has identified one {cite:parnia2014,parnia2023}. In 1948 a British psychologist published messages in cipher and promised to send the key after he died. He died in 1984. No medium has produced it, and two of the ciphers have since been broken by computer {cite:thouless1948}. A psychiatrist at the University of Virginia left a combination lock on the same plan {cite:stevenson1968}. Tests of mediums under blind conditions are split: one laboratory reports results above chance, a well-designed British study found none, and a pooled analysis finds a small effect that most psychologists do not accept {cite:beischel2015,okeeffe2005,sarraf2021}.

Steven said in advance that this kind of test is the hardest, because exact information is what comes through worst. A skeptic will call that moving the goalposts. It may also be an honest report of what he ran into. I can't tell which, and it means a miss proves nothing and a hit proves very little.

So I am going into this knowing that whatever happens will not be evidence for anyone else, and barely for me. I have decided that is not a reason to skip it.

## The list I am starting from

I am writing this down so that I can be held to it.

1. Talk to him, the way he said. Bring to mind what it was like to be around him. Ask first. Say it. Be polite. This cannot show that anything is received. It is also what a great many grieving people do, and it helps them.
2. Ask at the edge of sleep, and write down what comes before doing anything else. There is fair evidence that asking shapes what you dream {cite:barrett1993}. It cannot tell a visit from a dream.
3. Keep a dated log, including the nights when nothing happens and the impressions that turn out wrong. Otherwise I will remember only the hits.
4. Ask for something I don't know that someone else can check. Write the question, and what would count as an answer, before asking. Decide beforehand how much either result will be allowed to mean.
5. Find out whether he left a sign: a phrase, an object, anything he told someone he would use. That cannot be made up afterward. If he left nothing, then that is the answer to that question.
6. If I ever sit with a medium, arrange it so that it can fail. Give a first name only. Say as little as possible. Record it, and have a friend mix the statements with ones from somebody else's reading before I score them. Set a budget first, and walk away from anyone who guarantees a result or needs more sessions {cite:aarp-scams}.
7. Don't do it alone. That is his advice: {q:08-transcendence|Everybody needs somebody.}
8. Stop if it is making my life worse. That is his too: {q:08-transcendence|there's no shame in calling it quits if it's making your life worse}.

## If you are grieving too

Talking to someone who has died is ordinary. Feeling that they are near is common, and it is not a symptom.

Grief itself sometimes needs help. If a year on most of your days are still taken up by intense pain or numbness, by the sense that life has no meaning or that part of you died, and you cannot get back to people and plans, that is a recognized condition and there are treatments that work for it {cite:apa-pgd,shear2005}. Thoughts of ending your life are a reason to get help now, not later.

And take his own warning seriously. He says this practice is {q:07-telepathy|somewhat dangerous territory, especially for people with schizophrenia or other schizophrenic disorders}, and that it is not for everyone.

## Calling

He said to ask first, and to be nice about it.

Steven, may we speak?

I said this once already, in public, the week you died, and by your account you may have got it then. I'll say it again anyway, since you told us that what you wanted from the people you had lost was to know they thought about you.

I love you, Steven. Vaya con Dios, buddy.

I'm going to keep talking. Give me a shout.
