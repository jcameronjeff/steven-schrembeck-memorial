## The fork

He says he bent a fork into a knot in front of five or six excited people at a retreat, and that they then did it too.

In January 1981 an engineer named Jack Houck began holding gatherings he called PK parties, on exactly this pattern: raise the excitement in the room, tell the cutlery to bend, let go. He ran hundreds and reported that about 85 percent of guests succeeded {cite:houck1983}. The novelist Michael Crichton, who trained as a doctor, went to one and wrote that the metal went soft in his hands and that nearly anyone could do it {cite:crichton1988}. Neither Steven nor Joshua mentions Houck.

So the experience is real and common. What it is, is another matter. Nobody has shown metal bending without ordinary force under controlled observation, and when children who claimed the ability were watched without their knowing, they used their hands and feet {cite:pamplincollins1975}. The plain account, that excited people bend the metal themselves and misjudge how hard they pushed, fits Houck's parties and Steven's retreat equally well. Five or six people saw it. They could be asked.

## Easier for everyone

His claim that each success makes the next person's easier, anywhere, is Rupert Sheldrake's hypothesis of 1981 almost word for word {cite:sheldrake1981}. He does not name Sheldrake. The hypothesis has no independently replicated support, and its best-known illustration, the "hundredth monkey," was withdrawn by the writer who popularized it {cite:amundson1985}.

There is an ordinary version of the same observation which is certainly true. People do what they have just watched someone do, and believe what the room believes.

## Wanting it for everybody

The old books are cautious about powers. Patanjali lists them and then calls them obstacles {cite:patanjali}. Joshua brings up a recent history of saints said to have flown, whose author treats the sworn testimony as something a historian has to take seriously, without saying that it happened {cite:eire2023}. Steven's attitude is neither caution nor wonder, and it is the most characteristic thing in the chapter: {q:05-see-wake-up-remember-3|I want superpowers, but the reason we're talking right now is I want everybody else to have them too.}

## What I wonder

Joshua's question, why try for this while you're alive, gets an answer that surprised me. It isn't about powers. It is that you keep what you become, and that the people who have gotten somewhere are here to help the rest. I have no idea whether that is true. It is the answer of someone who thought the purpose of knowing a thing was to give it away, and that part I can vouch for.
