## Others who have stood here

The center of this chapter is a state, and the state is not rare. Being everything in your field of experience, with the sense of a separate self gone, is described in most contemplative traditions and has begun to be studied in laboratories {cite:josipovic2012,metzinger2024}. In 1902 William James listed the marks of such states: they can't be put into words, they don't last, they happen to you, and they feel like knowing {cite:james1902}. Steven's version of the last mark is {q:02-reality-101|This isn't a thing you can be told.}

What caught me is his reaction. He isn't blissful. He is let down, and then lonely: {q:02-reality-101|I'm the only one here. I'm always the only one here. I'm just sock puppets with myself.}

I went looking for that, and the nearest thing I found is in one of the oldest Upanishads. Its account of creation opens with a Self that looks around, sees nothing but itself, is afraid, has no delight, and wants a second {cite:brihadaranyaka}. The feeling is the same. The way out is different. In the Upanishad the Self divides in two. Steven stays one and makes a decision: {q:02-reality-101|I decide that it matters, just in that moment.}

A Zen teacher has a line for the letdown, that before you attain it it is something wonderful and afterward it is nothing special {cite:suzuki1970}. That is near, but it isn't the same thing. Suzuki is deflating the wish for attainment. Steven's disappointment is about being alone.

He had not read much of this. He says of that night: {q:02-reality-101|Coming into this, I had not done a lot of study on esoteric stuff, traditions.}

## The two lessons

"Awareness precedes control" has an ordinary cousin. In the 1960s it was shown that if you let a person see and hear the firing of a single motor unit in their own muscle, a signal that was always there, they can learn to fire it at will {cite:basmajian1963}. Becoming aware of the signal is what makes the control possible. That finding is solid, and it stops at your own body. Steven's claim does not stop there.

The shared library is close to the Theosophists' Akashic record, which Joshua names, and to Rupert Sheldrake's proposal that once something has been done it becomes easier everywhere {cite:akashic,sheldrake1981}. Sheldrake's book was called pseudo-science in the journal *Nature*, and his idea has no accepted replication. There is also a plain explanation for two people having the same idea at once: they were standing on the same pile of earlier ideas {cite:merton1961}.

What I have not found anywhere else is Steven's version of the library, which is not eternal. People wrote it, and keep writing it. {q:02-reality-101|So nobody's life is ever wasted. You've always made some contribution.}

## What I wonder

The state is real and can be trained. The step from "everything feels like me" to "everything is made of me" is an inference, and James's point was that this kind of knowing carries authority for the person who has it and none that anyone else is obliged to accept. I can't take that step on his word.

What I keep coming back to is the choice. A man who has just been shown, as he believes, that nothing outside him exists, and who decides in the same minute that the people in his life are real and that they matter, has told me something about himself that doesn't depend on whether the lecture was true.
