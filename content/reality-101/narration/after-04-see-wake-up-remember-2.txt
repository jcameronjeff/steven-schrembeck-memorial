## An old letter

For months he repeated three words he says he was given: {q:04-see-wake-up-remember-2|You need to see. You need to wake up. You need to remember.}

There is a poem in the Acts of Thomas, an early Christian text written in Syriac, usually called the Hymn of the Pearl. A king's son is sent to Egypt to bring back a pearl. He eats the local food, forgets who he is, and falls asleep. His parents send him a letter. In one standard translation it tells him to rise up and awake out of sleep, and to remember that he is a son of kings. In another it tells him to see whom he is serving {cite:hymnpearl_james1924,hymnpearl_wright1871}. All three of Steven's verbs are in that letter. Neither he nor Joshua mentions the poem anywhere in the series.

The idea that we arrive having forgotten is older still. In Plato's story of Er, souls about to be born drink from the river of forgetfulness {cite:plato_republic}. Joshua half-remembers this in the last episode, and he has it right. A school of philosophy in medieval Kashmir called itself Recognition, on the view that freedom is recognizing what you already are {cite:lawrence_iep}. Steven: {q:04-see-wake-up-remember-2|It feels like remembering, because it feels familiar.}

## A hole where a head should be

On April 8th the experience comes while he is awake: {q:04-see-wake-up-remember-2|You're not a boy in the world. You're the entire world with a boy shaped hole in the center.}

In 1961 an English writer named Douglas Harding published a short book about the day he looked for his own head and found, where it should have been, the scene in front of him. He calls it "this hole where a head should have been," and says it was perfectly simple {cite:harding1961}. As a description, that is as close a match as I found for anything in this book.

Harding claims no teachers and no powers. He also does not report what Steven reports next, which I have found nowhere else. The things around him seemed to know: {q:04-see-wake-up-remember-2|And I could feel that it loved me.}

One smaller thing. In February he has an image of himself as a ray of light sent through a row of prisms, each one a narrower self. William James used the prism in 1898 for the idea that the brain might not produce consciousness but shape and limit it {cite:james1898}. A group of researchers has revived that view; it remains a minority one {cite:kelly2007}.

## What I wonder

Whether the three words came from outside him, or from a mind that had spent years reading and meditating its way toward them, I can't tell. I doubt he could have either.

What I notice is the cost. This chapter starts with a depression, a job, and a computer that won't unlock. He did the repetitions for two months with nothing coming back. Whatever happened in the hammock, it happened to someone who had worked for it and who tells you how bad the working was.
