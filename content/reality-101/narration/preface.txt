Steven Schrembeck was my friend and my mentor. He was a principal engineer at Cox Automotive, a writer, a maker of audio fiction, and the person I trusted most to tell me whether a piece of work was any good. The first time I asked him a question, about machine learning, he answered within the hour with his own code and a list of doors not to knock on. He died in September 2026.

In his last year he made a podcast called *Reality 101*. On it he says, plainly and with a lot of jokes, that lights in the sky cracked the worldview he had built his life on; that he then taught himself to do things he used to make fun of; that he came to know people who are not physical, among them a sister and a mother; and that one night at the end of December someone showed him how reality works. He says anyone can do what he did, and he spends hours explaining how.

I am an engineer. So was he, and a better one. He describes himself before all this as {q:01-prologue|a person who is very orderly and logical}, and that is the person I knew. So I can't wave this away as the sort of thing that sort of person says. I can't just believe it either. What I can do is take it seriously, which is all he asked of anyone.

I have three reasons for making this book. I'll give them in the order of how easy they are to admit.

The first is other people. He made the series so that, as he puts it, {q:02-reality-101|people can understand their own reality and make sense of it, but also learn how to do it yourselves}. If something like this is happening to you, I want you to have his account whole. I also want you to know how many other people, across how many centuries, have come back describing the same country.

The second is that I want to understand what happened to my friend, so that I can work out how to keep what he started growing.

The third is selfish. If he is right, he isn't gone, and he left instructions. One of his last recordings ends: {q:07-telepathy|give me a shout one day. Maybe I'll hear you.} I want to know how.

## Two voices

His words are the body of every chapter. Nothing has been added to them, and the next section explains how that was checked. My pages are set apart and carry my name. In them I do three things.

I narrate: where we are in his story, and what to watch for.

I connect: who else has reported what he reports, from antiquity to last year, with the sources so that you can check me. For each comparison I try to say plainly whether the resemblance is close or loose, and whether he had read the thing I am comparing him to, because a man who has read a book and then has the experience in it is a different kind of evidence from a man who hasn't.

And I speculate, under headings that say so.

What I don't do is tell you it is true. I don't know. He had a rule for this, which I have tried to keep: {q:13-alien-spirit|I insist on testing weird stuff, but also accountability to skepticism and humility and some openness at the same time, I think.} He called it math and magic.

You should also know that I had help from an AI system: in cutting the transcripts, in finding and checking the sources, and in drafting my own pages. A program verifies every line of his against the transcripts of the recordings. The judgments are mine, and so are the mistakes.

He ended his episodes the same way each time. I'll borrow the line to begin: {q:00-invitation|nothing is impossible, you are not going to die, and everything loves you}.

Let's see.
