## The warning comes first

He begins by telling some listeners not to try: {q:08-transcendence|If you are not in a stable place in your life, you are not mentally or emotionally well, if you, pardon my French, can't handle your shit, then you should not yet embark on this journey.}

The research agrees with him. The Brown University study of meditators found that intensive practice can bring fear, loss of the sense of self, and perceptual disturbances that can last, in people with no psychiatric history as well as in people with one {cite:lindahl2017}. Stanislav and Christina Grof gave the name spiritual emergency to an opening that comes faster than a person can absorb it, and argued it should be supported and not treated as illness by default {cite:grof1989}. There are organizations that do this work now, some of them run by the researchers.

He also says the thing those organizations say, in his own words: {q:08-transcendence|You need to talk to somebody. You need to not feel alone in this.}

## Acting as if

The center of his method is to treat an experience as true and act on it before you are sure. An anthropologist would recognize this. Tanya Luhrmann's account of how members of evangelical churches come to hear God describes the same steps: take inner impressions seriously, behave as though someone is there, and keep doing it {cite:luhrmann2012}. Her later work across five countries found that the people most likely to experience invisible others are those who are easily absorbed in inner experience and who hold that minds are open to outside thoughts {cite:luhrmann2021}.

That finding cuts both ways and she says so. It may be how people learn to perceive something real. It may be how a mind makes company for itself. The method cannot tell you which, because it works either way.

## What the old books add

He recommends the *Yoga Sutras*, and his description of the central technique is a fair one. The text itself carries a caution he doesn't pass on, that the powers which come with concentration are obstacles to its real aim {cite:patanjali}. His three basic practices, wanting to know, sitting every day, and following intuition, are close to what most contemplative traditions would say, and so is his insistence that the work is done in ordinary life.

## What I wonder

I trust this chapter more than the lectures, because nearly everything in it can be checked against a life. Work on your problems. Stay in your responsibilities. Don't push it on people. Help. If the metaphysics is wrong, a person who followed this list would still end up kinder and steadier.

The part I can't check is the promise at the top, that the list ends in the realization he describes. He says himself that it rests on a sample of one.
