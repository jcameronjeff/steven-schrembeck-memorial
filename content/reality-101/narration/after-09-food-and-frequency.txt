## An old idea in a new costume

He says more than once that he had never heard this before. It is in fact one of the oldest ideas about food. A widespread Hindu teaching holds that the state of mind of the cook goes into the meal and affects whoever eats it, and that food offered with devotion is changed by the offering {cite:iskconprasada}. The Bhagavad Gita sorts foods by the quality they carry and pass on {cite:gita17}. Saying grace belongs to the same family.

That he arrived at it in a dream about a cooking show, from a teacher who introduces himself with a pun, is very like him.

## What has been tested

A researcher he had read, Dean Radin, ran small trials in which chocolate or tea was "treated" with good intentions and people who consumed it, without knowing which they had, reported better mood {cite:radin2007,shiahradin2013}. These are small studies from one group, and nobody independent has repeated them.

There is good ordinary evidence for something nearby. People experience food as tasting better, and pain as hurting less, when they believe it was given with care {cite:gray2012}, and performing a small ritual before eating makes the food more enjoyable {cite:vohs2013}. None of that needs the food itself to change.

One correction. He says Wim Hof had pathogens injected. In the published study the volunteers were given a bacterial toxin, not live bacteria, and those trained in Hof's method had a milder reaction {cite:kox2014}.

Two things here are more careful than the popular talk about "high vibration" foods.

The first is the definition. He pushes back with arsenic, made lovingly, and gets an answer I had not seen put this way: frequency is whether something is good within a context. The second is the instruction that comes with it: {q:09-food-and-frequency|do not harm others by misleading them into believing, basically, that you can turn something bad for you into something good for you.}

## What I wonder

Of everything in this book, this is the lesson that costs least to try. Cook for someone with attention. Notice how you feel after you eat. You don't have to settle what frequency is to find out whether he was onto something about dinner.
