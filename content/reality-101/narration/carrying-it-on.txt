Steven did not leave a monument to look after. He left a claim, a method, and a request that people check. So the question of how to keep his work alive has a practical answer, and most of it is work. This is the list I am working from. Some of it I can do alone. Some of it is not mine to decide.

## Keep the record whole

Everything in this book comes from recordings and writing preserved in the Steven Schrembeck Archive, with checksums, so that a reader thirty years from now can listen to the sentence I quoted and decide whether I cut it fairly. The archive still needs independent copies in places that will outlast any one host. That is unglamorous, and it comes first.

## Fix the text

The chapters were cut from machine transcripts. The editors flagged about two hundred places where the machine probably misheard him, and the list, with the time of each in its recording, is kept with the book's files. Every one of them needs a person with headphones. Most are small. A few could reverse what he meant, and those were left as transcribed or left out; nobody guessed. If you knew his voice, this is a way to help that takes an evening.

## Find the rest

In the first lecture he says: {q:02-reality-101|I've taken journal notes, like 200 something experiences at this point.} He says he drew what he saw in the sky straight afterward. He calls the two long journals in this book parts three and four of a miniseries, and in both of them he seems to be talking to a camera. I don't know what parts one and two were, or whether any video survives.

All of that belongs to his family. What happens to it, and when, is theirs to decide. I mention it only because a careful reader will wonder.

## Ask the people who were there

This book has one witness in it, and he is a good one. Joshua Swenson pushes back, tries things himself, and says so when they don't work. But there were others: the teacher whose class the two of them met in, the trainers Steven learned remote viewing and mindsight from, the people at the retreat where he says he bent a fork and then others did, and Sev, who knew him as a client before she interviewed him.

Their accounts, in their own words and checked the way his are checked here, would be worth more than any commentary of mine. The archive already accepts contributions and reviews them before anything is published.

## Do what he asked

In the trailer he says: {q:00-invitation|And if it's true, it should be verifiable.} In the transcendence journal he says that everything he recommends rests on {q:08-transcendence|a sample size of one}, and that {q:08-transcendence|it would be great to have a larger sample size}.

The most faithful thing anyone can do with this book is add to the sample. That means people trying what he describes and writing down what happened, including when nothing did. For the parts that can be checked from outside, it means checking them properly: the conditions agreed beforehand, someone skeptical choosing the controls, and the misses published with the hits. The chapter called *The Map* says where the existing research on each of these stands, and it is not where an advocate would want it to be. That is a reason to do the work well, not a reason to skip it.

I would rather his legacy be a hundred honest attempts than a thousand people who simply agree with him.

## Finish what he was building

Before any of this he was working on a plainer problem: how a person who cannot rely on willpower can still run their own life. Part Three is mostly about that. He built tools for it, first for himself and then for other people. I want to find out what state they are in and whether they can be kept available, because that work helped people whether or not anything else in this book is true.

## Keep this book correctable

My pages are a first draft by someone still finding his way in this literature. They will be wrong in places. The book is built so that it can be rebuilt: the sources are listed, the quotations are checked by machine, and every cut is on record. If you can show me a closer parallel, a better counter-argument, or a mistake, I want it.
